Overview

During my internship at the University of Granada, I worked within the institution’s communication and media unit, contributing to day-to-day audiovisual production across multiple formats.

My work included filming and editing interviews with faculty and researchers, covering institutional and cultural events, and producing content for academic and outreach communication channels. I also supported the organisation and management of audiovisual material within the university archive.

Rather than a single defined project, this experience consisted of continuous production work shaped by different briefs, deadlines, and departments within the university.

This experience helped me develop consistency in production workflows and adaptability across fast-changing institutional priorities.

What I learned

Working inside an institution like the University of Granada taught me things that no single project brief could. The scale, the variety of formats, and the need to serve many different audiences at once pushed me to be more adaptable and more organised than I'd been before.

It also showed me that good audiovisual work in institutional contexts isn't about being flashy — it's about being reliable, consistent, and clear. The work has to communicate before it impresses.

01
Working across formats
Switching between a sit-down interview, an outdoor event, and an archive task in the same week demands flexibility in how you set up, shoot, and edit. No two briefs were the same.
02
Adapting to institutional communication
Institutional content has its own tone — measured, credible, and accessible. Learning to work within those parameters without the work feeling flat was a real exercise in editorial judgment.
03
Storytelling with limited material
You don't always get the perfect shot or the ideal interview answer. Learning to build something coherent from what you have — and knowing when to ask for a second take — made me a better editor.
04
Content organisation at scale
Managing a media archive with hundreds of files across months of production taught me that good naming conventions and folder structures are genuinely important — not just admin.
05
Working under real deadlines
Event coverage needs to be out the same day. Interviews have to be edited before the news cycle moves on. The internship made deadlines feel concrete, not abstract.
